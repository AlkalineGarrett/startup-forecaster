field-crew::adoption_rate · distribution = weighted_combination

How the uniform band and the Beta body combine

A weighted_combination is a weighted blend of two sub-distributions (a mixture distribution, in the usual stats sense). Here: a near-zero uniform band for the 20% who structurally never adopt, and a Beta(2,24) body for the 80% who do. They combine by weight — both as a sampling recipe and as a sum of densities.

uniform [0.003, 0.012] · weight 0.20 Beta(2,24) · weight 0.80

Two ways to read “combine”

  1. As a recipe (how you'd sample it). Flip a weighted coin: with probability 0.20 the firm is a structural non-adopter — draw its share from uniform(0.003, 0.012) (essentially nil). With probability 0.80 it's an adopter — draw from Beta(2, 24). One draw, one component.
  2. As a density (how the curves add). The weighted_combination's density is the weighted sum of the two component densities: f(x) = 0.20·uniform(x) + 0.80·beta(x). At any x you add the two (weighted) heights — which is why the near-zero band shows up as a tall amber spike, and the rest is the teal Beta hump.
The allocation's constraint: "sum(components[].weight) == 1" is exactly what makes this a valid blend: 0.20 + 0.80 = 1, so no probability is created or lost — the two pieces partition all the mass.

The picture

Top: the combined density. The amber band is drawn to the top of the frame with an arrow because its true height (≈ 22) is far off this scale — that's 20% of the mass squeezed into a 0.9%-wide sliver. Bottom: the combined CDF. Read it left to right: across the tiny amber band the curve jumps from 0 to 20%, then the Beta body carries it the rest of the way to 100%. Percentiles are read straight off this curve.

        "high": 0.012

      "weight": 0.20

      "name": "adopting body",

    "constraint": "sum(components[].weight) == 1",
